Grammar

Vocabulary

Past Simple

When do we use it?

For actions that are finished at a specific point in the past.

Structure

  • Affirmative: subject + verb in the past (regular -ed / irregular)
  • Negative: subject + didn't + infinitive
  • Question: Did + subject + infinitive?

Time markers

yesterday · last week/year · in 2010 · two days ago · when...

Examples

  • We packed our bags and set out before sunrise.
  • She saw a giant tortoise on the trail yesterday.
  • They didn't build the campfire before it rained.
  • Did you hitchhike to the campsite?
Past Continuous

When do we use it?

For an action in progress at a moment in the past, often interrupted by a shorter action (in the past simple).

Structure

  • Affirmative: was/were + verb-ing
  • Negative: wasn't/weren't + verb-ing
  • Question: Was/Were + subject + verb-ing?

Time markers

while · as · when (+ interruption) · at that moment · at 8 o'clock yesterday...

Examples

  • We were hiking along the trail when we saw an endangered species.
  • I was reading the signpost while my friend was putting up the tent.
  • They weren't hitchhiking; they were waiting for a bus.
💡 Tip: the longer action (in progress) goes in the past continuous; the shorter action that interrupts it goes in the past simple.
Present Perfect

When do we use it?

For past actions without a specific time, but connected to the present: life experiences, current results, or actions that started in the past and continue now.

Structure

  • Affirmative: have/has + past participle
  • Negative: haven't/hasn't + past participle
  • Question: Have/Has + subject + past participle?

Time markers

ever · never · just · already · yet · since · for · so far

Examples

  • I have never seen a poisonous snake in real life.
  • Have you ever gone backpacking alone?
  • We have just found the perfect campsite.
  • She hasn't planned her route yet.
  • They have lived in this protected area for ten years.
Used to

When do we use it?

For past habits or states that are no longer true — there's usually a contrast with "now".

Structure

  • Affirmative: used to + infinitive
  • Negative: didn't use to + infinitive
  • Question: Did + subject + use to + infinitive?

Examples

  • I used to be scared of animals with claws, but not anymore.
  • We used to go camping every summer when we were kids.
  • He didn't use to like backpacking.
  • Did you use to have a pet turtle?
⚠️ Don't confuse this with "be used to" (to be accustomed to something) — that's a different structure.
